JUDGMENT

B.R. GAVAI, J.

1. This appeal is directed against the judgment and order dated 20.03.2022 passed by the High Court of Patna in Criminal Appeal No. 287 of 2020 whereby the High Court confirmed the conviction of the appellant under Section 304B read with Section 498A of the Indian Penal Code for dowry death and cruelty by husband and relatives.

BRIEF FACTS:

2. The factual matrix of the case is as follows: The marriage of the deceased Sunita Devi with Rajesh Kumar, the husband, took place on 15th March 2018. The appellant Priya Devi is the mother-in-law of the deceased. Within two years of marriage, on 10th January 2020, the deceased was found dead under suspicious circumstances at the matrimonial home. The post-mortem report revealed death by burns covering 85% of the body.

3. The FIR was lodged by the father of the deceased, PW-1 Munna Lal, under Sections 304B, 498A, and 34 of the IPC. According to the prosecution, the deceased was subjected to continuous harassment and cruelty for dowry demands of Rs. 5 lakhs and a motorcycle. The deceased had complained to her parents about the dowry harassment on multiple occasions.

4. The Trial Court convicted the appellant along with the husband under Section 304B IPC and sentenced them to 7 years imprisonment. The High Court confirmed the conviction.

ARGUMENTS:

5. The learned counsel for the appellant submitted that the appellant is an old woman of 62 years and was not present at the house on the date of the incident. There is no direct evidence of dowry demand by the appellant. The dying declaration was not recorded in the prescribed format and was unreliable. The conviction based solely on presumption under Section 113B of the Evidence Act is unsustainable.

6. The learned counsel for the State argued that Section 304B creates a legal presumption of dowry death when death occurs within 7 years of marriage under unnatural circumstances and it is shown that the deceased was subjected to cruelty for dowry. PW-3 and PW-4, the neighbors, deposed about hearing quarrels regarding dowry. The dying declaration was recorded by the Executive Magistrate and is reliable.

7. Having heard both sides and examined the evidence carefully, we proceed to analyze the merits of this appeal.

8. For an offence under Section 304B IPC, the prosecution must establish: (a) death by burns or bodily injury occurring otherwise than under normal circumstances; (b) within seven years of marriage; (c) it is shown that soon before her death, the woman was subjected to cruelty or harassment by her husband or his relatives in connection with demand for dowry.

9. In the present case, the death occurred within two years of marriage by 85% burns — clearly not under normal circumstances. However, we need to examine whether the evidence of cruelty and dowry demand is satisfactorily established.

10. The dying declaration was recorded by PW-8, Executive Magistrate Shri R.K. Mishra, who certified that the deceased was in a fit mental state. The deceased clearly named the appellant and her husband as the persons who set her on fire after she refused to bring Rs. 5 lakhs from her parents. This dying declaration is corroborated by the letters written by the deceased to her parents (Exhibit P-3 and P-4) complaining about dowry harassment.

11. PW-3 and PW-4, independent neighbors, testified about hearing frequent quarrels in the matrimonial home regarding dowry. PW-1, the father, produced evidence of having already given Rs. 2 lakhs and a television at the time of marriage.

12. The defense that the appellant was not present is contradicted by PW-5, the milkman, who saw the appellant at the house at 7 AM on the date of the incident. The death occurred at approximately 9 AM.

13. In view of the overwhelming evidence — the dying declaration, corroborative letters, independent witness testimony, and the statutory presumption under Section 113B of the Evidence Act — we find no infirmity in the conviction recorded by the courts below.

FINAL JUDGMENT:

14. The appeal is hereby DISMISSED. The conviction of the appellant under Section 304B read with Section 498A IPC and the sentence of 7 years rigorous imprisonment is UPHELD. The appellant shall surrender within two weeks to serve the sentence. Bail bonds stand cancelled.

15. However, considering the age of the appellant (62 years), we recommend that the appropriate authority may consider her case for remission as per the jail manual after she has served the minimum period.
