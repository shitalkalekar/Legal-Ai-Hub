JUDGMENT

D.Y. CHANDRACHUD, J.

1. The present appeal is directed against the judgment dated 18.05.2021 of the High Court of Allahabad confirming the conviction of the appellant under Sections 307 and 326 of the Indian Penal Code for attempt to murder and voluntarily causing grievous hurt by dangerous weapons.

BRIEF FACTS:

2. The prosecution case is that on 5th November 2019, at approximately 8:30 PM, the appellant Anil Sharma attacked the complainant Vikram Singh with a sharp-edged weapon (dao) at village Rampur, District Lucknow, causing multiple incised wounds on the head, neck, and arms. The complainant was rushed to King George Medical University Hospital where he was admitted in critical condition for 45 days. An FIR was registered under Sections 307, 326, and 504 of the IPC at Aliganj Police Station.

3. The motive for the attack was a dispute over agricultural land measuring 2 bighas which both parties claimed ownership of. There was a prior history of litigation between the families spanning over 15 years.

4. The Trial Court convicted the accused under Sections 307 and 326 IPC and sentenced him to 7 years rigorous imprisonment. The High Court confirmed the conviction and sentence.

ARGUMENTS:

5. The learned senior counsel appearing for the appellant contended that the injuries caused were simple in nature and did not indicate intention to cause death. The medical evidence shows only superficial wounds. There was no injury to any vital organ. The conviction under Section 307 IPC was not made out.

6. The learned Additional Solicitor General appearing for the State submitted that the nature of the weapon used, the number of blows inflicted, and the location of injuries on the head and neck clearly demonstrate the intention to kill. PW-2, Dr. Meena Gupta, the treating physician, categorically stated that the injuries were dangerous to life. Three independent eyewitnesses (PW-3, PW-4, PW-5) corroborated the prosecution version. The recovery of the blood-stained dao at the instance of the accused further strengthens the prosecution case.

7. We have carefully considered the rival submissions and gone through the evidence on record.

8. In determining whether an offence under Section 307 IPC is made out, the court must examine the nature of the weapon used, the number of injuries, the force applied, and the part of the body targeted. In State of M.P. v. Kashiram (2009) 4 SCC 26, this Court held that where the accused inflicts injuries on vital parts of the body with a deadly weapon, the intention to cause death can be presumed.

9. The medical evidence of PW-2 establishes that the complainant sustained 7 incised wounds — 3 on the head, 2 on the neck, and 2 on the arms. The head injuries required 28 stitches. The doctor categorized the injuries as "dangerous to life." The complainant was in ICU for 12 days.

10. Three independent eyewitnesses consistently deposed about the incident. Their cross-examination did not reveal any material contradiction. The weapon of offence was recovered at the instance of the accused under Section 27 of the Evidence Act. The forensic report confirmed human blood on the dao matching the complainant's blood group.

11. Having regard to the nature of the weapon (a 14-inch dao), the number of blows (7 cuts), the targeting of vital areas (head and neck), and the severity requiring 45 days hospitalization with ICU admission, we have no hesitation in holding that the prosecution has established the charge under Section 307 IPC beyond reasonable doubt.

12. The conviction under Section 326 IPC is also fully warranted given the nature of grievous hurt caused.

FINAL JUDGMENT:

13. The appeal is DISMISSED. The judgment of the High Court confirming the conviction and sentence under Sections 307 and 326 IPC is hereby UPHELD. The appellant shall surrender to custody within four weeks to serve the remainder of his sentence. The bail bonds, if any, stand cancelled.

ORDER:

The appeal is dismissed. The conviction and sentence are upheld. Allowed partly only to the extent that the sentence under Section 504 IPC is set aside as the charge was not proved. The substantive sentence of 7 years RI under Section 307 IPC is maintained.

Wait — upon reconsideration, this Court notes the appeal is dismissed on all substantive charges. The conviction stands. This appeal has no merit and is rejected.
