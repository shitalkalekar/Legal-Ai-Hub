JUDGMENT

S. RAVINDRA BHAT, J.

1. This appeal arises from the judgment of the High Court of Madhya Pradesh dated 22.08.2021, whereby the High Court reversed the decree of the Trial Court in a suit for partition and separate possession of joint Hindu family property.

BRIEF FACTS:

2. The appellants are the widow and daughters of late Shri Ramesh Chandra who died intestate on 15th March 2019. The respondents are the brothers of the deceased. The suit property consists of ancestral agricultural land measuring 25 acres situated at Village Sehore, District Sehore, Madhya Pradesh, and a residential house in Bhopal.

3. The appellants filed a suit for partition claiming a one-third share in the joint family property. The deceased Ramesh Chandra was one of three brothers. The appellants contended that as legal heirs of the deceased coparcener, they are entitled to his share in the joint Hindu family property under the Hindu Succession Act, 1956, as amended by the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005.

4. The Trial Court decreed the suit, holding that the appellants are entitled to a one-third share. The High Court reversed the decree, holding that the property was self-acquired by the respondents' father and the appellants had not established coparcenary rights.

ARGUMENTS:

5. The learned counsel for the appellants submitted that revenue records (Exhibit P-1 to P-12) clearly show the land as ancestral property owned by the joint family for four generations. The mutation entries show the names of all three brothers as co-owners. The respondents themselves admitted in their written statement that the family had been living jointly until 2015.

6. The counsel further submitted that under Section 6 of the Hindu Succession Act, as amended in 2005, daughters have equal coparcenary rights. The widow of a deceased coparcener is entitled to his share by intestate succession. The High Court erred in reversing the well-reasoned decree of the Trial Court.

7. The learned counsel for the respondents submitted that the property was purchased by their father Shri Govind Prasad from his own earnings as a government contractor. The sale deeds (Exhibit D-1 and D-2) show purchase in the individual name of Govind Prasad. The mutation in the names of sons was done only for convenience of management.

8. We have examined the rival contentions and the documentary evidence.

9. The core question is whether the suit property is ancestral/joint family property or self-acquired property of Govind Prasad.

10. Upon examination of the revenue records spanning 1965 to 2019, we find:
(a) The original land was owned by Shri Babu Lal (grandfather) as recorded in the revenue records of 1965.
(b) After Babu Lal's death in 1978, the land was mutated in the name of Govind Prasad and his brother Mohan Lal.
(c) The so-called "sale deeds" (Exhibit D-1 and D-2) dated 1982 show purchase of only 8 acres of additional land — not the entire 25 acres.
(d) The remaining 17 acres have been continuously recorded as ancestral property since 1965.

11. The respondents' own admission in their written statement that "the family lived jointly and shared agricultural income until 2015" is a judicial admission that cannot be retracted. This establishes the joint family character of at least the ancestral 17 acres.

12. The High Court committed a manifest error in treating the entire 25 acres as self-acquired when the documentary evidence clearly establishes that 17 acres are ancestral and only 8 acres were subsequently purchased.

13. Under Section 6 of the Hindu Succession Act, the deceased Ramesh Chandra had a coparcenary interest in the ancestral property. Upon his death intestate, his share devolves upon his heirs — the appellants — by survivorship and succession.

FINAL JUDGMENT:

14. This appeal is ALLOWED in part. The judgment of the High Court is SET ASIDE. The decree of the Trial Court is restored with modification:

(a) The appellants are entitled to a one-third share in the ancestral property of 17 acres.
(b) With respect to the 8 acres purchased by Govind Prasad, the appellants are entitled to one-third of the share that would have devolved upon Ramesh Chandra by intestate succession from his father.
(c) The residential house in Bhopal, being built from joint family funds on ancestral land, shall be treated as joint family property, and the appellants are entitled to a one-third share therein.

15. A preliminary decree for partition is passed. The Trial Court shall appoint a Commissioner for demarcation and partition within 12 weeks. Costs shall be borne by the parties in proportion to their shares.

16. The appeal is allowed. Ordered accordingly.
