JUDGMENT

SANJIV KHANNA, J.

1. The present appeal challenges the judgment of the High Court of Rajasthan dated 14.11.2022 confirming the conviction of the appellant under Section 420 read with Section 120B of the Indian Penal Code for cheating and criminal conspiracy.

BRIEF FACTS:

2. The prosecution case is that the appellant, along with two co-accused (since acquitted), operated a fraudulent real estate scheme in Jaipur between 2017 and 2019, collecting approximately Rs. 3.2 crores from 47 investors by promising plots in a non-existent housing colony called "Royal Paradise Township." The FIR was registered under Sections 420, 467, 468, 471, and 120B of the IPC at Mansarovar Police Station, Jaipur.

3. The Trial Court convicted the appellant and sentenced him to 5 years rigorous imprisonment with a fine of Rs. 10 lakhs. The co-accused were acquitted for want of evidence. The High Court confirmed the conviction of the appellant.

ARGUMENTS:

4. The learned counsel for the appellant strenuously argued that the appellant was merely an employee — a marketing manager — working under the directions of the co-accused who were the actual promoters. The appellant had no signing authority over the company bank accounts. He did not receive any proceeds of the fraud. The acquittal of the principal accused creates serious doubt about the appellant's conviction.

5. Furthermore, several complainants in their cross-examination admitted that all dealings were with the co-accused and not the appellant. The documents relied upon by the prosecution, including brochures and receipts, do not bear the appellant's signature.

6. The learned counsel for the State submitted that the appellant was the face of the operation who personally convinced and lured investors through presentations. Multiple witnesses identified the appellant as the person who collected cheques and gave false assurances. The appellant's phone records show 2,400+ calls with the investors during the relevant period.

7. We have heard both parties at length and examined the record.

8. The essential ingredients of Section 420 IPC are: (i) deception of any person; (ii) fraudulently or dishonestly inducing the person to deliver property; and (iii) the accused must have a dishonest intention at the time of making the promise.

9. Upon careful review of the evidence, we find merit in the appellant's contention. The following facts are established from the record:

(a) The company "Royal Paradise Developers Pvt. Ltd." was incorporated and owned by co-accused Sunil Meena and Dinesh Agarwal, who have been acquitted.
(b) The appellant joined as a salaried employee (Marketing Manager) in January 2017 with a monthly salary of Rs. 25,000.
(c) All bank accounts were operated exclusively by the co-accused. The appellant had no signatory powers.
(d) PW-12, PW-18, and PW-23 admitted in cross-examination that all payment receipts and agreements were signed by the co-accused, not the appellant.
(e) The appellant himself filed a complaint against the co-accused in 2019 for non-payment of his salary arrears.

10. When the principal conspirators and actual beneficiaries have been acquitted, convicting the employee who was merely executing marketing duties — without evidence of sharing criminal intent or proceeds — would be a grave miscarriage of justice. We rely on Devender Kumar v. State of Haryana (2014) where this Court held that knowledge of the fraudulent nature of the scheme must be established.

11. There is no evidence that the appellant knew the housing colony did not exist or that the scheme was fraudulent. He appears to have been as much a victim of the co-accused's fraud as the investors.

FINAL JUDGMENT:

12. In light of the above analysis, this appeal is ALLOWED. The conviction of the appellant under Section 420 read with Section 120B IPC is SET ASIDE. The appellant is ACQUITTED of all charges. The appellant, if in custody, shall be released forthwith unless required in any other case. The fine amount, if deposited, shall be refunded.

13. The appeal is allowed. The impugned judgment of the High Court is set aside.
